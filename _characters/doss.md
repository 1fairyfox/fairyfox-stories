Doss has tended the model for so long that the tellers forget it is a person and not a fixture — the old
airkeeper who reads the *Ark Meridian*'s honest breath, the true measure of what the sealed world is
actually breathing, drinking, eating, and warming. Where the Muster keeps the register of who is *named*,
Doss keeps the record of what the ship *feels*, and it is the quiet friction between those two numbers
that has held for four hundred years — until it doesn't.

## What Doss knows

That the margins are wider than the creed admits. Doss has watched the model carry its steady, unnamed
extra draw for years without a single shortfall — no lean season tipped, no harvest failed — and has
said nothing, because the saying is not an airkeeper's to do. The one-for-one quittance, Doss has long
understood, is stricter than the ship's survival actually requires; it is kept strict because a strict
rule is *holdable* and a humane one is a judgement someone has to make.

## Doss's part in the tale

Doss is the crack in the creed and the foil to Halden: the proof that the sealed world had more mercy in
it all along than its keepers would spend. Reluctant, honest, and tired, Doss gives Wren the one thing
that turns her certainty — the record that says the overcount cost no one anything — and lets her decide
what to do with a truth an airkeeper was never supposed to hand a teller.
