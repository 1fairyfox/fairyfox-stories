Provost Halden has kept the Muster for longer than Wren has been alive, and he keeps it the way a man
keeps a wall he believes is the only thing between everyone he loves and the cold: without apology and
without rest. He is courteous, exact, and genuinely kind in the small ways; he learned the names of the
tellers' children and remembers them. He is not a miser of air. He is a man who has read the founders'
sum and believes it to the body.

## What he believes (and it is not nothing)

That the strict count is the reason no one on the *Ark Meridian* has starved in four hundred years — that
a sealed world survives only on a rule you can hold *exactly*, and that the moment the count admits one
merciful exception it invites a hundred hidden births and unravels the discipline that keeps everyone
fed. To Halden, strictness is not cruelty; it is the shape mercy has to take in a jar with no lid. He is
right about the stakes. A sealed world *can* famine.

## Where he is wrong

He has, for years, half-suspected an old overcount in the books — and chosen not to look, because
looking risks the very order he protects. Order over inquiry; the wall over the one behind it. He is
Wren's own creed carried one step further than she can follow: caution grown so complete it has become
the thing it was built to guard against. His argument must be answered, not dismissed — which is exactly
why answering it costs Wren everything she was.
