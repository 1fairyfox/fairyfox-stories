Wren Astel is twenty-four and keeps the books of the *Ark Meridian* — a junior teller of the Muster,
the office that holds the sealed ship balanced to the body: one aboard for every one the ship can bear,
a birth licensed only against a death. She inherited the post, and the fair copperplate hand, and the
creed, from her mother, who kept the register thirty years and has been two years dead. She strikes a
name from the living register with a single clean line, and it steadies her rather than shakes her, and
she has always taken that steadiness for strength.

## What she wants (and needs)

- **Wants:** to close every column — to keep the books perfect, as her mother did, as the ship has for
  four hundred years. To be the teller who reconciled the gap no one before her could.
- **Needs:** to learn that the count she was raised to call mercy has been kept crueler than survival
  requires; that a ledger which cannot make room for a soul already breathing keeps *order*, not lives;
  and to find the harder courage of deciding a record is *for* someone.

## The flaw that costs her

She mistakes order for justice and precision for love — her mother's lesson, worn as her own: *precise
is a kind of love the sad ones never learn.* An unbalanced column frightens her more than cruelty does.
That is exactly the lever the Muster, and her own inheritance, pull on when the books turn up one body
they cannot name.

## Voice

First person, **present tense**, close and exact; she notices numbers, columns, the draw of a body on a
sealed world, and keeps her warmth rationed under the procedure until it forces its way up. Self-aware
about everything except this.
