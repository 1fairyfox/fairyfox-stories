## Premise (one line)

On a sealed generation ship that licenses one birth per death, a proud young teller of the population
count strikes a beloved name and finds the ship still breathing for one body more than the register can
name — a twin hidden at birth eighteen years ago, alive her whole life in the unmapped cold decks — and
must choose between the ledger she was raised to call mercy and the hidden life at the end of it.

## Form & structure (novella — one conflict, one change, deep)

- **One central conflict** (Wren vs. the count she believes in, incarnated as a hidden person she must
  decide the fate of), **one transformation** (order → mercy, at real cost). No wandering subplot; the
  only secondary threads (Ondine's grief, Doss's margins, Halden's fear, Sela's map) all feed the main
  line.
- 11 chapters, each a scene/beat with its own small arc, feeding the larger one. Rising stakes; a breath
  before each turn (present tense is relentless — the quiet beats are load-bearing).
- Chapter-length guide (not a formula): ch.1–9 ~1,600–1,900; ch.10 ~2,000; **ch.11 ~2,800** (finale
  ~1.5× — the turn *and* the falling-action/resolution both land as scenes).

## Author-persona & style (HOLD for the whole book)

- **Aster Coyle** (`_authors/aster-coyle.md`): taut, procedural, human SF. Clipped concrete declaratives;
  ship-nouns used precisely and unglossed (the reader learns the world by watching it work, not by
  lecture); emotional restraint that cracks late and briefly. Warmth kept *under* the surface —
  released at chosen moments (the tomato, the light decks), never smeared over the prose.
- **Present tense, first person, held for the whole book.** This is the book's signature and its main
  distinctness lever on a shelf of past-tense narrators. Guard the two present-tense failure modes:
  breathlessness (vary sentence length; let scenes settle) and "I"-stacking (start on the object, the
  number, the ship — not on *I*).
- **Deliberately unlike recent books:** last plants were Salt Road fables (Adem — spare fable; Okri —
  exuberant fable) and a Hollow Hours magical-realism novella (Idris). This is cool propulsive SF —
  and distinct from the realm's own *Cartographer* (Idris: cool, meditative, past-tense survey) by being
  a present-tense **thriller/hunt** with a living person at its end.

## POV & voice

- First person, present tense, close. Wren narrates as a teller: she notices numbers, columns, the fair
  hand, the draw of a body on a sealed system. Her precision is characterization; her creed ("precise is
  a kind of love") is her mother's voice worn as her own. **Keep a live blind spot:** she believes the
  strict count is mercy and calls her own fear of disorder "prudence" / "precision." Let the daylight
  between what she says (order = love) and what's true (order ≠ mercy) carry the tension. She is
  self-aware but not *about this* — not until the book makes her be.

## Main character — Wren Astel (want vs. need vs. flaw)

- **Wants:** to close the impossible column; to keep the books perfect as her mother did; to be the
  teller who reconciled the ungapfixable gap.
- **Needs:** to see that the count has been kept crueler than survival requires; that a ledger that can't
  make room for a breathing soul keeps order, not lives; to find the courage to decide a record is *for*
  someone (the realm's through-line, inherited from Sela and paid at a higher, public price).
- **Flaw that bites:** mistakes order for justice, precision for love; more frightened of an unbalanced
  column than of cruelty. The system and her own inheritance pull exactly that lever.
- **Agency:** she opens the inquiry (ch.2), walks the cold decks (ch.3), files the sighting (ch.5, the
  cost-free wrong), meets Ilex (ch.7), refuses the correction and pays the forfeit of her post (ch.10).
  Every major turn is her choice and its price.

## Cast

- **Ilex** (`_characters/ilex.md`) — the overcount; 18; the hidden twin; raised unseen in the cold
  decks. Careful, quick, strange with solitude; not feral. Wants to keep breathing, to protect Sabine's
  memory, and to stand once in the light beside Ondine. **Not** a thief — a person hidden the day he was
  born. Release his interior in small pieces; he is a mystery before he is a boy.
- **Ondine Verrine** (`_characters/ondine-verrine.md`) — the registered twin; 18; grow-deck apprentice;
  grieving Sabine; doesn't know (or half-knows) she was two. The living face that makes the column human;
  the reunion in ch.9 is the emotional hinge.
- **Provost Halden** (`_characters/provost-halden.md`) — head of the Muster; the institutional
  antagonist, **right about the stakes, wrong about the only mercy.** Sincere, formidable; has
  half-suspected an old overcount and chosen not to look. His argument must land (make him the Vera
  Brandt of this book — correct about the rules, wrong about this). No cartoon miser of air.
- **Doss** (`_characters/doss.md`) — old airkeeper who tends the model; uneasy, honest; proves the
  ecology carried the overcount without harm (the crack in the creed). The foil who embodies mercy the
  system had all along and its keepers wouldn't spend.
- **Sabine Verrine** — the twins' mother; grow-deck steward; dies (attended, off-page) in ch.1; gave a
  child-Wren a warm tomato once (the book's emotional Chekhov object — pay it back at the light decks).
  No separate page; lives in canon and in Ilex's/Ondine's pages.
- **Sela Renn** (`_characters/sela-renn.md`, reuse) — the cartographer of the parent tale; now keeps a
  private honest chart of the off-map decks. **One scene** (ch.3): gives Wren the true map and a warning;
  recognizes a fellow keeper at the same threshold. The sequel tie + within-realm crossing. **A nod, not
  a subplot** — reads standalone (a first-timer sees a wise cartographer; a *Cartographer* reader gets
  the full rhyme). Do not re-reveal *Cartographer*'s ending; imply it at most.

## Long Quiet canon to honour (`_universes/the-long-quiet.md`)

- Hard-ish, human-scaled SF: no aliens, no FTL. Sealed ecology; the wonder is scale, time, and the slow
  mystery of a machine older than memory. **Cerebral, not gory** — the realm bar.
- Established by *Cartographer* (do not contradict, do not re-reveal): the *Ark Meridian* is **coasting**
  and the **Purpose is a kind lie**; the **Cartographers' Office** edits the master charts, generation to
  generation, as a mercy. Wren's fight is the **count**, not the destination — keep the Purpose-lie in
  the deep background (a whisper in Sela's scene at most).
- **New canon this book adds (annotate the codex):** the **Muster** — the office that keeps the ship
  balanced to the body; the **quittance** (a death frees a share; a birth is licensed against it); *the
  count is the mercy.* These are additive and consistent with the ration/sealed-ecology canon.
- Texture: recycled air and grow-lamp violet; the hum of the turning hull; numbered decks, laminated
  charts; the cold of the dark/outer decks; ritual, rationing, routine; the tally-lamps' cold blue.

## The engine (mechanics to keep exact)

- **One aboard for every one the ship can bear** — the founders' first law; the count's whole reason.
- **The quittance** — a death frees one body's share; a birth is licensed against it. Sabine's death
  frees a clean adult share (no dependents) in ch.1 → **paid off ch.10**, when Wren licenses **Ilex
  against Sabine's own quittance.** The mother's death makes room for the son the mother hid. This is the
  Chekhov gun; keep it visible in ch.1 and untouched until ch.10.
- **The overcount = twins on one licensed birth.** Sabine bore two 18 years ago, entered Ondine, hid
  Ilex. The register only ever knew one; the model always ran high by one. That is why the gap is exactly
  one and exactly 18 years old.
- **The reconciliation** — register (named living) vs. the model (the ship's honest breath/water/food).
  Always equal — until ch.1. A sealed world can't hide a body; the model feels the draw. This is *why*
  the overcount is discoverable at all, and why it took a death on a clear-drift night to surface it.
- **The complicity beat (ch.5):** Wren glimpses Ilex, files the sighting by protocol, tightens the net —
  every fact in hand, the reward (a clean closed column) real, and it costs her nothing, and she can't
  unsee the face. The realm/shelf signature (`craft/character.md`); do it freshly, no monster needed.
- **The turn (ch.10):** not out-tricking Halden — **paying the count's own forfeit openly.** Wren
  licenses Ilex in the light and files her own complicity; loses her post. Care over order — a public
  price, higher than Sela's private one.
- **Restraint:** the "correction" of an unlicensed body is **implied, off-page, bureaucratic-cold** — a
  quitting of the leak, a cold the ship won't look at. Never depicted. Menace, not gore.

  - **The tomato:** child-Wren (age 9) sat under Sabine's grow-lamps; Sabine gave her a **warm tomato off
    the vine — "the first thing I ever ate that no one had rationed to me"** — never told anyone, "not on
    any roll." (Emotional Chekhov object → pay back at the light decks, ch.11.)
  - **The reconciliation:** register (named living) vs. the model (the ship's honest breath). Always
    equal — until now. After striking Sabine the model runs **high by one whole body.** Wren hunts
    backward through the cold logs overnight and finds the gap is **not** Sabine and **not** drift: it
    runs high by exactly one for **as far back as the logs stay fine — more years than she's been alive**
    (i.e., 18+; the exact figure stays soft in ch.1, sharpened in ch.2/4). An unlicensed body, born to
    the voyage, never entered — **"a leak"** in the Muster's creed.
  - **Frame/voice:** first person, **present tense**, close; ends on the thematic question ("Who has been
    breathing? And what, precisely, is the Muster for — if not for them?"). Wren names her taught alarm
    (raise-the-hall, seal-the-decks) and the small warm question under it that she'll spend the book
    avoiding, then answering.

## Pitfalls to avoid (this book specifically)

- Don't let Halden become a cartoon miser — his stakes are real (a sealed world *can* famine); make his
  case land so Wren's choice costs.
- Don't solve ch.10 with a trick or a hack of the register — Wren must **pay openly** (the growth, and
  the realm's care-over-order theme, at a public price). No clever loophole.
- Keep the "correction" off-page and cold; keep Sabine's death gentle and attended. Register: **gripping
  before sad** (`genre-range.md`) — a hunt/thriller with a warm center, not a grief-soaked downer.
- Present tense is relentless — vary rhythm, land quiet beats before turns, don't stack "I"-openings.
- No infodump of the ship — release mechanics on need-to-know beats, in Wren's teller-voice (ch.1's count
  exposition is deliberately voiced as her creed; keep later worldbuilding smaller and in motion).
- **Do not re-reveal *The Cartographer of Decks*'s secret** (the coasting ship / Purpose-lie). Keep it
  background; Sela's ch.3 scene may *imply* the ship keeps merciful fictions, no more. This book reads
  fully standalone.
- Hold Aster Coyle's taut procedural voice — resist the fable cadences of the recent Salt Road plants and
  the cool meditation of *Cartographer*; this is a present-tense hunt.
