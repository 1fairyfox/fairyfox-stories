### The chapter

**Ch.3 "The First Try" (~1,760 words)** — the first of the fable's three tries, by **mastery**, and
the map's brief is "mastery fails; the coat opened; the stopper taken; the wind turns onto his road."
Craft decisions:

- **The reasoning that makes the failure earned.** Basri's whole life's stand (be the most
  interesting *shut* thing in the street — the ch.1 "a bottle is a shut thing turned inside out: all
  door") is precisely wrong for a wind that opens shut things: "to bait a wind that opens shut things
  with a shut man is to offer a fox a fox." So he reaches for the one thing he trusts — his craft, a
  *name*. He reads what the wind does and builds it the grandest true-name forty years can raise:
  **"Opener,"** unlatcher of the world. The vanity beat named plainly: "pride is only fear that has
  combed its hair," and "he mistakes the thing he is afraid of for the thing he has not yet solved."
- **The name is wrong for the right reason — but the reason is guarded.** He names what the wind
  *does*, brilliantly; it is wrong because the Unbottled's name is not a wind-name at all (the ch.2
  trade-lore: "the thing you will not say"). The chapter lets him *half-see* it at the very end ("the
  name was never out there to be listened for — it was in the pocket") without saying what the pocket
  holds. **Nuru's name/kinship stays a ch.6 reveal** — I caught and softened a first-draft line that
  leaked "a boy and his sister" and cut it to "the towns where I came up young into the trade."
- **The turn — the wind opens his coat.** No violence (kept to the realm's restraint bar): "there was
  no violence in it… it came in against my breast the way a hand goes into a coat it has every right
  to," past the purse and the estate-worth contract, and takes the **one** thing — the cracked horn
  stopper (planted ch.1, hand-beats held ch.2). First rung of the escalation the book-notes set:
  **coats → pages (ch.4) → milestone (ch.6) → him → the vault (ch.7).** He scrambles in the dust after
  it — the great Basri Yel undignified before a child and a market — and the motif inverts: the
  catcher's hand that "goes to the true thing while the mouth is busy" now finds the pocket empty and
  "did not know what to do with itself."
- **Escalation paid.** The wind **breaks its hundred-year circuit** and runs *his* road west, toward
  the towns of his young years — set up in ch.2's close ("does not yet know my scent") and now
  triggered. Sets the pursuit into his own country and ch.4's trickery-try.
- **Noor kept consistent + foil landing.** Her ear is *trained, not magic*: she "cannot hear that one"
  because it was never in glass, so it never knocked (coheres with ch.2's "they knock… all the time").
  Her throat-tell carried; her ch.2 "it doesn't choose" paid back inside Basri's dread. Her flat
  correction — "a good name, not a true one" — rhymes with ch.2's "that is a law about you" without
  repeating it.
- **Voice/length.** ~1,760 words (ch.1 ~2,000, ch.2 ~1,930; map ~1,850 — a hair under, a craft call;
  first draft was 1,538 and I added the ride/certainty beat to seat the fall). Okri music held:
  cadence that swells then drops to a plain line at the true beats ("It opened my coat."), rule of
  threes, sly warmth at Basri's expense, the bottles-and-names motif.
