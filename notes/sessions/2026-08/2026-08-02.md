### The book

*The Overcount.* Generation-ship SF **thriller**, **present tense** (a first for the shelf — every other
narrator speaks in past; a deliberate distinctness lever, held whole-book). Wren Astel, 24, junior teller
of the **Muster** — the office that keeps the sealed *Ark Meridian* balanced to the body, one birth
licensed against one death (a freed **quittance**) — strikes a beloved name and finds the reconciliation
won't close: the ship breathes for **one body more than any register can name.** The overcount is
**twins on one licensed birth** — Sabine bore two eighteen years ago, entered Ondine, hid **Ilex** in the
cold unmapped decks. Wren hunts him, and the realm's/shelf's **complicity beat** (ch.5) lands cleanly:
she glimpses him, files the sighting by protocol, tightens the net, and it costs her nothing and she
can't unsee it. The turn (ch.10): she doesn't out-trick the Muster — she **pays its own forfeit openly**,
licensing Ilex against **Sabine's just-freed quittance** (*the mother's death makes room for the son the
mother hid*) and taking the fall herself. Antagonist **Provost Halden** is right about the stakes (a
sealed world *can* famine) and wrong about the only mercy; **Doss** the airkeeper proves the ecology
carried the overcount 18 years without a shortfall (the crack in the creed). **Sela Renn** gets the
sequel-tie: one ch.3 scene giving Wren the true map of the off-chart decks — a nod for *Cartographer*
readers, required by none; *Cartographer*'s secret is **not** re-revealed. Full public blueprint shipped;
ch.1 "The Quittance" (~2,000 words) written to Coyle's taut procedural present-tense voice, planting the
quittance engine + the warm-tomato Chekhov object.
